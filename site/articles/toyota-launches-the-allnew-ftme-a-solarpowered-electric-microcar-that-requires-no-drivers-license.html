Toyota Launches the All-New FT-Me: A Solar-Powered Electric Microcar That Requires No Driver’s License
advertisement

Toyota has recently unveiled a brand-new all-electric microcar—the Toyota FT-Me. This compact vehicle is packed with innovative technology and boasts a remarkable 90% reduction in carbon emissions compared to conventional electric cars. Could this be the future trend for urban short-distance travel?

Measuring just 2.5 meters in length, the FT-Me falls under the "microcar" category in Europe, placing it in the same price range as the Citroën Ami (approximately £7,695). However, unlike traditional cars, the FT-Me is made from 90% recyclable materials and features a solar panel on the roof, which can provide an additional 30 km of range per day, making it an environmentally friendly choice.

In addition, the FT-Me eliminates the need for a traditional car key, relying entirely on smartphone control. Owners can even set user permissions—for example, parents can restrict their children’s driving time, enhancing safety management.

In many European countries, teenagers as young as 14 years old can drive the FT-Me, as its power does not exceed 8 horsepower, its weight is under 425 kg, and its maximum speed is limited to 45 km/h. This regulation allows young drivers to experience safe and independent mobility before reaching the legal driving age for standard cars. However, the UK is not included in this category.

The FT-Me is not only ideal for young drivers but also highly accessible for people with disabilities. Its steering wheel is equipped with manual acceleration, deceleration, and braking controls, eliminating the need for pedals or additional modifications—making it easy for wheelchair users to drive. Although the car’s windows are large, they cannot fully open. Instead, a camera-based rearview system enhances safety.

Toyota aims to keep the FT-Me’s price in line with the Citroën Ami (£7,695), targeting consumers looking for a second car for daily commutes and young individuals who have not yet reached the legal driving age for standard vehicles but seek independent mobility. In most European countries, 14-year-olds and older can legally drive the FT-Me, as it meets EU microcar regulations (power under 8 horsepower, weight under 425 kg, and a top speed of 45 km/h). However, this does not apply to the UK.

Toyota promises an affordable price, low maintenance costs, and the best resale value in its class. As a second vehicle or short-distance commuter car, the FT-Me presents an attractive and practical option.
