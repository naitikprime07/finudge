The Controversy of Overhyping "Autonomous Driving" and Accident Responsibility: Who Is Liable When an Accident Occurs with Autonomous Driving Activated?
advertisement

With the rapid development of intelligent driving technology, an increasing number of car manufacturers have introduced various driver assistance and autonomous driving functions. The popularity of these functions has not only changed the way people travel but has also brought new challenges, especially in terms of liability determination and insurance coverage. In recent years, as advanced intelligent driving technologies have become more widespread, accidents caused by consumers misunderstanding the distinction between "driver assistance" and "autonomous driving" have increased, leading to widespread discussions and controversies.

Firstly, regarding the issue of liability for accidents involving "autonomous driving," there are currently no clear legal regulations. Traditional car accident liability determination typically relies on driver negligence, such as drunk driving, fatigue driving, or violating traffic rules. However, the emergence of intelligent driving systems has made liability more complex. Intelligent driving involves multiple fields, including hardware, software, and data services, meaning that responsibility may be distributed among several parties. For example, if the intelligent driving system is defective, the manufacturer or seller may be held responsible for product liability; if data is tampered with, the data service provider could face liability. Due to the wide range and complexity of the involved industries, the process of holding parties accountable is relatively difficult, and current laws have not provided clear guidelines on this issue.

Another important reason for frequent accidents is the consumer's misunderstanding of intelligent driving technology. Many people mistakenly think that Level 2 driver assistance systems are "fully autonomous driving" and neglect the fact that these systems still require the driver to remain alert and control the vehicle. This misunderstanding has not only led to the misuse of the technology but has also caused fatal accidents. Therefore, clarifying the boundaries between "driver assistance" and "autonomous driving" has become a pressing issue for the industry.

In the future, as intelligent driving technology becomes more widespread, it will become an important development trend in the automotive industry. It is expected that from 2025, the scale of intelligent driving products will rapidly expand. This change will bring new challenges to the automobile insurance market. Currently, insurance products related to intelligent driving face many difficulties. Traditional car insurance pricing is mainly based on factors such as the vehicle's value and the driver's habits, but intelligent driving cars need to consider new factors like hardware reliability, software security, and data confidentiality. Because the data provided by car manufacturers is inconsistent and cannot be fully accessed, insurance companies find it difficult to assess risk accurately, affecting the pricing of insurance products.

Moreover, there are significant differences in the sensors and hardware devices used by different companies, and the volume of test data is enormous and inconsistent in format, making data organization and risk assessment more challenging. Additionally, the continuous updates and iterations of autonomous driving algorithms cause insurance claim data to be unstable, further complicating the pricing process for insurance companies.

Therefore, while pushing for technological innovation, car companies must balance "technological innovation" and "safety promotion." On one hand, car companies should increase consumer education to ensure that they correctly understand the actual functions and limitations of intelligent driving technology. On the other hand, car companies should enhance technological transparency, provide reliable data support, and ensure the safety of intelligent driving technology. Only through clear publicity and standardized technological development can misunderstandings be avoided while promoting the healthy development of the technology.

For consumers, whether to trust this kind of technology under strict limitations on driver assistance functions is still a question worth considering. Although intelligent driving technology performs excellently in some areas, under conditions where safety and stability are not fully guaranteed, complete manual control may still be a choice trusted by many. With the continuous development of technology and the improvement of laws, insurance, and other aspects, intelligent driving may provide a safer and more reliable mode of travel in the future.

In conclusion, the popularization and development of intelligent driving technology will bring many new issues. How to balance innovation and safety, how to regulate liability determination and insurance coverage, are urgent problems for the industry to solve. Only by ensuring the safety and transparency of the technology can intelligent driving truly mature and gain consumers' trust.
